Welcome to Ibadan School for the Deaf

Over Six Decades of Pioneering Deaf Education

From a humble beginning with four pupils in 1963 to a landmark residential and day educational institution in Ijokodo, Ibadan.

Ibadan School for the Deaf was established in 1963 by Late Chief (Mrs.) Omodele Adelaide Oyesola (M.O.N., J.P.) together with her husband, Late Chief Jacob Oluseye Oyesola, initially as the Home for the Young Deaf in a garage at Ososami Road, Ibadan. Driven by compassion, faith, and specialized training at Gallaudet College (now Gallaudet University, Washington D.C.), the founders opened doors of learning to deaf children at a time when very few opportunities existed.

Following a land donation at Ijokodo by the Anglican Diocese of Ibadan — facilitated with the support of the World Council of Churches — and classroom, dormitory, audiology, and vocational contributions from Oyo State Government, the Federal Government of Nigeria, Rotary Clubs, the Commonwealth Society for the Deaf, and international partners, the school grew into one of Nigeria’s foremost centres for the education and rehabilitation of hearing-impaired learners.

Today, former pupils of Ibadan School for the Deaf serve across Nigeria and abroad as teachers, university and college lecturers, engineers, administrators, civil servants, and skilled entrepreneurs.

Our Vision

To provide the best education possible for hearing-impaired children, giving them opportunities to develop physically, intellectually, emotionally, and socially to their fullest potential — both inside the classroom and in day-to-day dining, sports, dormitory, and curricular life.

  • Inclusive communication through sign language and speech support
  • Strong academic foundations and moral guidance

  • Active partnership with parents and families
Our Founding Heritage

From the Founder’s Desk

Extracts from the 40th Anniversary reflections of our founder, Late Chief (Mrs.) A.O. Oyesola (M.O.N., J.P.).

Founder & First Headmistress

Late Chief (Mrs.) A.O. Oyesola

M.O.N., J.P. · Iya Abiye of Ibadanland

“

Sowing the Mustard Seed: The seed was sown in faith in 1963 with only four pupils in a garage donated by Late Mrs. Onabamiro at SW9/950 Ososami Road, Ibadan. In spite of initial difficulties, God remained faithful and upheld the work in service to deaf children.

The Growth of the Seed: Through the appeal of the World Council of Churches, the Anglican Diocese of Ibadan donated land at Ijokodo for a permanent campus. Dedicated Nigerian humanitarians, civic clubs, churches, government leaders, and international partners rose up to construct classrooms, boys’ and girls’ dormitories, a multi-purpose hall, computer classrooms, and an Audiology Clinic.

Beyond the Ijokodo campus, Mama Oyesola worked alongside eminent Nigerian educators to establish Special Education teacher training at the University of Ibadan, served on the formative Governing Council of Federal College of Education (Special), Oyo, and championed supportive services for deaf students at the University of Ilorin (UNILORIN).

Why Choose Us

Education Beyond the Classroom

Rooted in our vision statement, every dimension of student life at Ijokodo is structured to support confidence, character, and independence.

Pioneer Deaf Education Heritage

Established in 1963 as a pioneer school for the deaf in Nigeria, with decades of institutional experience guiding hearing-impaired learners into teaching, engineering, management, and public service.

Specialist Teaching & Speech Support

Dedicated educators trained in deaf education combine sign language, visual pedagogy, and speech preservation so every pupil can communicate clearly and learn without stigma.

Supportive Boarding & Dormitories

Boys’ and girls’ hostel facilities offer a caring home away from home for pupils travelling from across Oyo State and beyond, nurturing daily routines and personal independence.

Vocational & Computer Training

Dedicated computer-training classrooms and practical vocational supervision help students acquire 21st-century digital and trade skills to build dignified, self-reliant careers.

Dining, Sports & Curricular Life

Shared dining halls, athletics, table tennis, cultural dance, and clubs foster teamwork, physical well-being, emotional resilience, and lasting friendships.

Audiology Clinic & Parent Guidance

Our historic Audiology Clinic and parent education outreach help families recognize early signs of hearing impairment, embrace sign language at home, and advocate for their children.
