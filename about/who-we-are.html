Who We Are

A pioneer school for the deaf in Nigeria, founded in faith in 1963 and committed to empowering hearing-impaired learners for a dignified, productive future.

Institutional Overview

A Venture of Faith That Opened Doors Across Nigeria

Ibadan School for the Deaf is one of Nigeria’s pioneer educational institutions dedicated to children with hearing impairment. Located at Polytechnic Road, Ijokodo Junction, Ibadan, Oyo State, the school began in 1963 as the Home for the Young Deaf with just four pupils in a borrowed garage at Ososami Road, Oke Ado, Ibadan.

From those humble beginnings, the school grew through the dedication of its founders, the collaboration of the Oyo State Government, and the generosity of local and international benefactors into a permanent campus offering Pre-Primary, Primary, Secondary (integrated pathways), Vocational Training, and supportive residential boarding care.

Our core belief remains unchanged: deaf children are not mentally deficient or limited in potential. With early intervention, sign language, speech support, quality teaching, and community inclusion, hearing-impaired learners achieve excellence alongside their hearing peers across every field of human endeavour.

Official School Vision

“The purpose for Ibadan School for the Deaf is to provide the best education possible for hearing-impaired children. The idea of providing the opportunities to develop the children physically, intellectually, emotionally and socially to their fullest potential cannot be over emphasised. Since education is not provided only in the classroom, the students are further trained broadly in their day-to-day activities: dining, sports, dormitory and other curricular activities.”

Guiding Principles

What Defines Our Educational Mission

Grounded in verified institutional records and our founders’ enduring philosophy.

Dignity & Equal Opportunity

Every deaf child deserves the same educational opportunities, respect, and family inclusion as hearing children, free from stigma or misconceptions.

Holistic Development

Learning extends beyond classroom desks into moral character, speech and sign communication, nutritious dining, dormitory life, athletics, and cultural expression.

Self-Reliance & Empowerment

By uniting strong academics with computer literacy and practical vocational skills, we equip graduates for higher education and independent livelihoods.

Our History

Milestones From 1963 to Today

Key documented chapters preserved in the school’s 40th and 50th Anniversary archives.

1963 · Sowing the Mustard Seed

Home for the Young Deaf Established

Following specialized training at Gallaudet College (now Gallaudet University, Washington D.C.) and brief work with deaf missionary Andrew Foster, Chief (Mrs.) A.O. Oyesola and her husband Chief J.O. Oyesola started the school as a “Venture of Faith” with four pupils in a garage donated by Late Mrs. Onabamiro at SW9/950 Ososami Road, Ibadan. It soon expanded as Home School for the Deaf at Agbeja Road, Oke Ado.

1967 · Permanent Site at Ijokodo

Land Donation & First Classroom Block

Through the appeal of the World Council of Churches, the Anglican Diocese of Ibadan donated permanent campus land at Ijokodo. Supporters including the American Women’s Group, Friends of the Deaf School Society, YWCA, and YMCA raised funds for the first classroom block.

1970s · Campus Expansion & Facilities

Dormitories, Audiology Clinic & Civic Support

Donations from the World Council of Churches, Christian Council of Churches in Nigeria, Christian Aid (London), Church of Canada, Bread for the World (Germany), Netherlands Children Used Stamps Group, Rotary Clubs, the Federal Government of Nigeria, and Nigerian leaders funded boys’ and girls’ dormitories, classrooms, a generator from Major General David Jemibewon, and an Audiology Clinic equipped with support from the Nigerian Youth Trust and the Commonwealth Society for the Deaf (Britain).

1973 – 1984 · National Impact

National Honours & Higher Education Advocacy

In 1973, Founder Chief (Mrs.) A.O. Oyesola was conferred Member of the Order of the Niger (M.O.N.) and Iya Abiye of Ibadanland. Alongside Nigerian special-education pioneers, she helped integrate deaf-education teacher training at the University of Ibadan, served on the formative Governing Council of FCE (Special) Oyo, and advocated for supportive deaf services at the University of Ilorin (UNILORIN). She retired voluntarily from Oyo State service in 1984.

50th Anniversary & Beyond (60+ Years)

Continuing Legacy of Service

With sustained Oyo State Government collaboration, alumni leadership, and community philanthropy, Ibadan School for the Deaf has educated thousands of hearing-impaired learners across pre-primary, primary, integrated secondary (Ijokodo High School), and vocational pathways.

Founder’s Biography

Remembering Late Chief (Mrs.) Omodele Adelaide Oyesola, M.O.N., J.P.

Founder (1963)

Chief (Mrs.) A.O. Oyesola

M.O.N., J.P. · Iya Abiye of Ibadanland

Born to the family of Late Pa Adeigbe Ishola (a Methodist missionary) and the Orokunle family of Olorisaoko in Ibadan, Omodele Adelaide Oyesola attended Methodist Primary School, Agbeni, Sagamu Girls School, and earned her Grade II Teachers’ Certificate at United Missionary College (UMC), Oke Ado, Ibadan (1948 Set).

Her lifelong calling into special education began during an annual Girls’ Brigade camp when she noticed a young deaf girl standing apart from other children. Moved by compassion, she visited the girl’s family and later travelled to Gallaudet College (now Gallaudet University) in Washington D.C. to study deaf education.

Alongside founding Ibadan School for the Deaf with her husband Late Chief Jacob Oluseye Oyesola (married in 1954), she authored sign-language instructional books, helped introduce sign language at Emmanuel College of Theology, chaired Community-Based Vocational Rehabilitation in Oyo State to help special-needs citizens start trades in shoemaking, catering, weaving, fashion design, and barbering, and served actively in Rotary International (Paul Harris Fellow, Past President of Rotary Club of Eleyele, and founder of Rotary Inner Wheel Club of Eleyele).
