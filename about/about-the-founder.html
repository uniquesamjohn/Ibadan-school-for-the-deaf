About the Founder

ABOUT THE FOUNDER

Our dearest mother, Late Chief A. O. Oyesola, MON, J.P., and her dearest husband, Late Chief J. O. Oyesola, jointly founded Ibadan School for the Deaf in the year 1963. Late Chief (Mrs) A. O. Oyesola, MON, J.P., the Iya Abiye of Ibadan, was born on 25th July 1930 to the family of Late Pa S. G. Ishola of Amule Compound, Foko and Mama Ajeigbe Ishola of Orokunle Family of Olorisaoko both in the ancient city of Ibadan. Her husband, Late Chief J. O. Oyesola, was born into the family of Late Chief and Chief (Mrs) N. O. Oyesola of Asaluyo Compound in Abeokuta. Papa and Mama passed on 1st October, 1998 and 13th September, 2011 respectively.

CAREER

After her graduation at United Missionary College, Molete, Ibadan, Chief (Mrs) A. O. Oyesola emerged as a qualified and trained teacher. Therefore, she was posted to Methodist Primary School, Oke-Ado, Ibadan to begin her career as a teacher. Her teaching career also took her to many schools in the old Western states of Nigeria. Similarly, her husband started his teaching career after graduating as a Grade II teacher. He attended University of Ibadan and then proceeded to Gallaudet College, Washington, U.S.A.

MARITAL LIFE

The Iya Abiye of Ibadan met her sweetheart, Late Chief J. O. Oyesola, in the early course of her career. The union was enhanced as the two love birds belonged to the same denomination, Methodist Church, where Papa was a member of Boys Brigade and Mama was a member of Girls Brigade. In the year 1954, the two were joined in a holy matrimony at Oke Ijemo Registry, Abeokuta, Ogun State and the union was blessed with five children namely Chief Sir Olujare Oyesola, Mrs Oluwabunmi Sobogun, Late Babajide Oyesola, Professor Oluwatokunbo Oyesola and the matron of the school, Mrs Temitope Oyesola, who has served meritoriously for twenty-five years and is still active. Papa and Mama were blessed with many grandchildren, great-grandchildren and non-biological children who were trained in various professions and vocations.

HISTORY OF IBADAN SCHOOL FOR THE DEAF

...Mama's Race into Special Education

Mama's venture into Special Education came by divine direction and inspiration. The journey began when she met a deaf girl named SELIATU in Ikere-Ekiti during one of her Girls Brigade camp. Mama was drawn emotionally to Seliatu when she came innocently to the camp to watch the proceedings but was rejected. Chief (Mrs) A. O. Oyesola subsequently followed her home to meet with the parents and also asked for permission to take Seliatu with her to Ibadan due to the emotional attachment. The parents of the girl were more than happy to release her as most parents of abled children seldomly want to identify with them. Mama travelled back with the girl to Ibadan to meet her husband, Chief J. O. Oyesola, who wholeheartedly accepted them. In a quest to learn how to further improve communication with Seliatu, Mama travelled to Gallaudet College (now Gallaudet University) in Washington D.C., United State of America which is the only university for the deaf in America then.

INCEPTION OF THE SCHOOL FOR THE DEAF

On her return from America, Mama's burden now moved beyond caring for a deaf child to all the deaf children in Ibadan and beyond. She worked briefly with Andrew Foster, a deaf missionary who was interested in bringing Christ to the deaf in Nigeria. Through the Mission, Mama familiarized herself with the deaf and her aspiration led to the establishment of the Home for the Young Deaf in 1963 which was tagged "VENTURE OF FAITH." The home started in a motor garage at Ososami, Oke-Ado, Ibadan with a girl and four boys. The Venture of Faith then grew to become Home School for the Deaf at Agbeja Road, Oke-Ado, Ibadan with over thirty (30) children.

By 1967, Home School for the Deaf got a facelift as Anglican Mission gave acres of land for a permanent site in Koloko, Ibadan and several bodies including American Women Group, friend of the Deaf Society, Young Women Christian Association (Y.W.C.A.), Young Men Christian Association (Y.M.C.A.) raised funds for the building of the first classroom blocks.

In 1970, series of donations from World Council of Churches, Christian Council of Churches in Nigeria, Christian Aid in London, Church of Canada, Bread for the World, Germany contributed to the growth of the school. Their supports aided in building more structures for the school such as dormitories, warden's house and boys quarters. The international gestures prompted the Federal and Western State governments to donate staff quarters to the School in recognition of her pioneering role in deaf education in Nigeria and to lay the foundation of Special Education in Nigeria and three secondary schools (Ijokodo High School I & II and Senior School) with facilities for the training and integrated education of the hearing-impaired and the normal children. Mama retired voluntarily from the service of Oyo State in 1984 as Head Teacher Ibadan School for the Deaf. The School is now popularly known as IBADAN SCHOOL FOR THE DEAF.

CONTRIBUTIONS TO HIGHER EDUCATION FOR SPECIAL EDUCATION

When the government took over all schools, deaf children in Western States were brought to Mama's school and meeting up with the required teachers for the deaf became a great challenge. At that time, only few specialized teachers were available but Mama rose up to the challenge. In conjunction with Professor S. A. Bakare, P. O. Mba, Rev. M. A. Awe, Alhaji Daodu, Mr Gbegbin, Dr Aderinola and B. O. Afolabi, Mama initiated the first national training course for training-impaired teachers into the University of Ibadan. The course later developed into a Diploma course which eventually led to the formation of Special Education Department in the faculty. Mama also produced sign language books to aid teaching and learning by the deaf and teachers in the training.

Mama was instrumental to the establishment of the Department of Special Education at the tertiary level when she found out that the deaf were finding it difficult to get admission into higher schools of learning. She was a member of the Board of Governors of the University of Ibadan, Ibadan, Oyo State, and through her, the Federal Ministry of Education set up the Federal Government's Special Education Center for the deaf at the University of Ilorin (UNILORIN) when it was no longer easy for the deaf to go to Gallaudet College as a result of high foreign exchange or naira to dollar. Today, qualified deaf persons can get admission into UNILORIN to study any course of their choice.
